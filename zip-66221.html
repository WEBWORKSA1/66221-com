What daily life feels like

66221 sits on the southern growth edge of Overland Park. Much of it was farmland a generation ago, so housing skews newer than the city's north end: two-story and reverse-ranch homes on generous lots, many inside HOA-managed subdivisions with pools, trails and clubhouses. Expect cul-de-sacs, three-car garages and finished basements to be common rather than special.

It is a driving community. Errands, school runs and commutes happen by car, and the main arteries — 135th, 151st, 159th and 167th Streets, Antioch Road, Quivira Road, Pflumm Road and US-69 — carry most of the traffic. Downtown Kansas City is roughly a 30–40 minute drive outside peak hours; the Corporate Woods and College Boulevard employment corridors are closer.

Who tends to live here

The numbers describe a household-dense, family-oriented, highly educated area. Nearly half of households earn high incomes, home ownership dominates, and a meaningful Asian-American community contributes to a more diverse population than much of Kansas. School quality is a major reason people choose the ZIP — see our schools page.

Trade-offs to weigh

  • Cost. Prices and property taxes are among the highest in the metro. Use the calculators before you shop.
  • Weather. Hail, wind and the occasional tornado warning are part of spring. Insurance costs reflect that — read Storm & hail ready.
  • Walkability. Trails are excellent, but daily errands on foot are rarely practical.
  • Growth. Ongoing construction brings new amenities and also traffic and road projects.
