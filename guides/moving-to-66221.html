Moving to 66221: the complete guide

By the 66221 Living Editorial Desk · Updated October 21, 2026 · 11-minute read

66221 combines newer housing, highly regarded public schools and quick access to the Kansas City metro's southern job centres. Here's what to know before you sign — and what to do in your first month.

What it's like

Expect quiet, well-kept subdivisions, many with HOA pools and walking trails, interspersed with shopping centres along the main arterial roads. The population is family-heavy — the average household has more than three people — and the area is highly educated, with more than four in five adults holding at least a bachelor's degree. Weekends revolve around youth sports, parks and the trail network.

Housing and costs

Most homes are single-family, built from the 1990s onward, with plenty of new construction on the southern fringe. The Census Bureau's ACS 2023 survey put the median owner-estimated home value at about $670,500, and recent sale medians have run higher — Redfin reported roughly $867,000 for July 2026. Monthly medians move around with the mix of homes sold.

  • Property tax: plan on roughly 1.4% of value a year. See our tax guide.
  • Home insurance: Kansas premiums are elevated because of hail; get quotes before you go under contract.
  • HOA dues: vary widely by amenities; read the covenants for fence, shed and exterior-colour rules.
  • Sales tax: combined rates in the area run around 10%. Kansas removed its state sales tax on food and groceries in 2025, though local sales taxes can still apply.

Schools

Most of the ZIP is served by Blue Valley USD 229. Elementary schools located inside 66221 include Sunset Ridge, Morse and Harmony; middle schools include Harmony and Aubry Bend. Confirm assignment by address — see our schools page and enrollment guide.

Getting around

66221 is car-dependent (Walk Score 17). US-69 runs north–south through the area and connects to I-435; east–west arterials include 135th, 151st, 159th and 167th Streets. Typical off-peak drives: 15–20 minutes to the College Boulevard/Corporate Woods corridor, 30–40 minutes to downtown Kansas City, and about 45 minutes to Kansas City International Airport. Johnson County Transit (RideKC) offers limited service, so most households plan for two vehicles.

Weather

Four real seasons: humid summers, cold winters with periodic snow, and a volatile spring with thunderstorms, hail and occasional tornado warnings. Basements are common and double as storm shelters. Read Storm & hail ready before your first April.

Your first 30 days

  • ☐ Transfer or set up electricity, natural gas, water, trash and internet — your closing agent or HOA can confirm which providers serve your street.
  • ☐ Register to vote with the Johnson County Election Office.
  • ☐ Get a Kansas driver's license within 90 days of becoming a resident, and title and register vehicles through the Johnson County Treasurer's motor vehicle offices.
  • ☐ Enroll children in school and arrange transportation.
  • ☐ Learn your trash and recycling day and any HOA rules on bins.
  • ☐ Sign up for city and county emergency alerts.
  • ☐ Photograph the house, roof and contents for insurance records.
  • ☐ Change the locks and garage codes; locate the water shutoff and main breaker.
  • ☐ Book HVAC service so you have a baseline on the systems you inherited.
