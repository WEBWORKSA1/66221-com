Selling a home in 66221

By the 66221 Living Editorial Desk · Updated October 21, 2026 · 9-minute read

66221 is a move-up market: buyers are often relocating families comparing school boundaries, finished basements and HOA amenities line by line. Sellers who understand that buyer win on price and speed.

1. Timing: when 66221 sells best

Family buyers want to close before the school year starts, so activity builds from late February, peaks in spring and stays strong into early summer. Listings that hit the market in March–May generally see the most showings. That said, fall and winter listings face less competition, and relocation buyers shop year-round. In mid-2026, Redfin reported 66221 homes going under contract in roughly 19 days at the median — a market that still rewards well-prepared listings.

2. Pricing: comps, boundaries and finishes

  • Match the school boundary. Comparable sales should share elementary and middle school assignments where possible; boundary differences can be worth real money.
  • Separate above-grade and basement space. Appraisers value finished basement square footage differently from main-level space.
  • Account for lot position. Backing to green space or trails often commands a premium; backing to a busy arterial road can cost you.
  • Price for the search bands. Buyers filter by round numbers. A home at $801,000 misses everyone searching "up to $800,000."

3. Pre-listing fixes that usually pay

Basement moistureClean sump pit, working backup and no efflorescence on walls.
Landscape and irrigationFirst impressions from the curb, especially in spring.

Skip major remodels right before listing — you rarely recover the full cost. A pre-listing inspection can surface issues on your timeline rather than the buyer's.

4. Commissions and costs after the 2024 changes

Following the 2024 national settlement involving the National Association of Realtors, offers of buyer-agent compensation are no longer published on the MLS, and buyers sign written agreements with their own agents. In practice, many sellers still choose to offer or negotiate buyer-agent compensation as part of the deal. Everything is negotiable — discuss strategy with your listing agent up front.

Beyond commissions, Kansas sellers typically pay for the owner's title policy (customary in the area, though negotiable), prorated property taxes, payoff and recording fees, and any agreed repairs or credits.
